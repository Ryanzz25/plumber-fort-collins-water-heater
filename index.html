Water Heater Repair in Fort Collins: A Quick Checklist

Most water heater problems show up as one of five symptoms. Here is what each one usually points to.

No hot water at all

On a gas unit, check whether the pilot is lit and the gas valve is on. On an electric unit, check the breaker. If both look fine, the thermocouple, igniter or a heating element may have failed.

Hot water runs out fast

Sediment settles at the bottom of the tank and takes up space that should hold water. Hard water in Northern Colorado makes this common. An electric unit with one failed element gives the same symptom.

Rumbling or popping sounds

That noise is water trapped under a layer of sediment, boiling as the burner heats it. Flushing the tank helps if it is done before the sediment hardens.

Rusty water or a leak at the base

Rust-coloured hot water can mean the anode rod is used up. Water pooling under the tank is more serious: a leak from the tank body itself usually means replacement, while leaks from fittings or the relief valve can often be repaired.

Safety first

If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility. Never cap or plug a dripping temperature and pressure relief valve; it is there to stop the tank from over-pressurising.
